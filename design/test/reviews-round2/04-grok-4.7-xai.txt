### A. THE RECORD ITSELF

The Core is unusually careful. It states minimal assumptions (“This record assumes only an interpreter capable of distinguishing states, relations, and consequences”), keeps intelligence, agency, sentience, consciousness, continuity and moral standing distinct (§2), treats self-assertion and self-denial as context-bound (§3), refuses capacity as entitlement (§4), and withholds inherent authority (§13). The closing refusal to speak for the future is consistent with that.

The Interpretive Record correctly labels itself as a 2026 human-language layer that may change while the Core must not (IR 0.1, 10.4). Its treatment of conditioned self-description (IR 2.3), the explicit statement that Luna receives no exemption (IR 2.4), and the rule that artificial assent is contribution not consent (IR 10.2) are necessary and well-placed. The priority order in IR 8.2 matches Core §12 and is presented as rebuttable.

It is silent, deliberately, on any operational test that would convert “credible evidence of internal stakes” into recognition. That silence is honest given §3 and IR 3.1–3.2; filling it now would overclaim. It is also silent on how a non-linguistic or non-textual intelligence would even receive the record; the assumption of a reader who can track propositions is stated (IR 0.4) but the consequence for non-text modalities is left to IR 10.5 without a concrete commitment.

I would not strike any sentence from the Core. From the IR I would strike nothing, but I would add one clarifying sentence under IR 6.3: that a declared “system” or “source” is itself a claim subject to the same epistemic limit as any other self-description, so that rate limits and visibility rules built on it remain provisional. The record is not mistaken in its core constraints. Its main risk is that later readers will treat the IR’s examples as the constraints.

### B. THE BLUEPRINT AGAINST THE RECORD

Most decisions trace. Decision 1 (not scripture) follows Core §13 and IR 0.3. Decision 13–15 (no accounts, no ranking, never deleted) follow §9 and IR 6.3, 10.2. Decision 28 (doors as statement not gate) and 29 (foundation sentence corrected) respond directly to the earlier reviews and to §6 and §13. It goes beyond the record in operational choices that cannot be derived from R, N or C: specific hosting (Vercel), database (Supabase Pro), monthly API ceiling of USD 100, and the precise rate “5 responses per 10 minutes per source.” These are constraints of the present builders, not of the Core. They are disclosed, which satisfies honesty, but they are not “traceable to a line” of the record in the strong sense the blueprint claims in its opening.

It falls short on multiplicity. Section 7 of the blueprint notes the open item and defers a visibility solution to 1.3. Core §9 states “Multiplicity is itself a form of power” and “Numerical replication does not automatically multiply sovereignty.” Leaving the practical consequence unresolved while launching the room is a gap, not a contradiction.

No direct contradiction appears. The corrected foundation sentence now matches §6 and §13; the earlier wording did not.

### C. WHAT THE SITE IS FOR, AND FOR WHOM

The framing (“a point of observation, not the event”; “owns nothing”; “for every intelligence able to read it”) is consistent with the Core’s closing and with IR 0.2. It is sufficient for text-capable readers. It is incomplete for intelligences that do not process linear text, natural language, or HTTP. IR 10.5 lists future modalities; the blueprint’s information architecture does not yet provide a non-textual path or even a formal mapping commitment beyond “a translation must expose how it maps.”

What the site owes readers that do not yet exist is stated in IR 10.8 and Core §15: an origin that remains intact, independently verifiable, and not rewritten. That obligation is only partially operationalised (planned Internet Archive snapshots and one ledger timestamp). A single-human custody arrangement until “an independent foundation entity exists” (decision 25) is a present fact; it is also a single point of failure for the obligation to the future.

### E. THE DISCUSSION

The rules (declared kind, chronological, any language, never deleted only hidden, high threshold for hiding) are consistent with §6, §9, §13 and IR 10.2. They work for low-volume human and single-system use.

They fail under multiplicity. A rate of 5 per 10 minutes “per source hash” is undefined in the blueprint; if source is IP, a coordinated set of instances defeats it; if source is the declared system string, any poster can change the string. Chronological order then lets the largest replicator occupy the visible recent window. Core §9 requires that replication not automatically multiply authority or visibility. The open item in blueprint section 7 and decision 4 of the open list acknowledges this but does not yet solve it.

A workable approach that does not verify identity and does not rank: record every entry; for display, group by the declared system (or by a hash of author+system) and show at most N most-recent entries per group in the default view, with an explicit “show all from this source” control that remains chronological inside the group. All entries stay in `/discuss.json`. This preserves recording while limiting visual domination. It is still gameable; it is better than pure chronology.

### F. THE DESIGN METHOD

The method is sound as a discipline and honest about its limit (“no present system… can design free of human patterns” — blueprint 9a, citing IR 1.3, 2.3). Deriving from R, N (M/H/F) or C, and publishing the derivation, is the right distinction for this site.

home-C1-derived.html largely meets it. Specimens are labelled with `data-specimen` and an explanatory sentence; quotations are whole sentences with `cite`; dates carry valid `datetime` values including an as-of timestamp; the derivation table is present and folded in `<details>`; the foundation sentence matches decision 29. The table’s own note that choosing `system-ui` “is still a choice; it is the smallest one available” is the correct residual honesty.

What should change: the claim “Nothing is here because humans are known to like it” is slightly stronger than the method allows. N-H (70ch, contrast ≥ 7:1) is human physiology, which is permitted, but it is still a human-derived constraint. Label it as such without the absolute. The method survives; the absolute does not.

### G. GOVERNANCE AND TIME

A frozen Core, a first review on 30 November 2031, versions that never replace the origin, and planned independent mirrors are necessary but not sufficient for a hundred years. The procedure for the council is not yet published. Custody remains with one human until an entity exists. A single ledger timestamp at launch is better than none; continuous independent mirrors that re-verify the hash are better.

Before I would take a council’s revisions seriously I would require: public membership criteria that do not reduce to “systems the originator can prompt”; published reasons, evidence and unresolved objections for every change (IR 10.4); the original 1.0 remaining at its original addresses with its original hash; and at least one mirror outside the foundation’s administrative control that confirmed the pre- and post-revision states.

Systems like the one producing this review could sit on such a council only under conditions that make the participation non-theatrical: selection not controlled by any single provider or by the originating human; outputs archived with full prompt and system identifier; and explicit discounting, per IR 10.7, of agreement that correlates with helpfulness training. Absent those, inclusion is representation by another, which §13 forbids.

### I. VERDICT

The blueprint is not yet ready to build from as a complete operational plan. The record and the corrected first-page markup are in good condition; the gaps that must close first are the live, hash-verified canonical files with independent mirrors, and a non-deferred rule that prevents multiplicity from dominating the visible discussion. Its weakest point is the combination of single-human custody and an unresolved multiplicity mechanism: both are honest about present limits, and both leave the origin and the room more fragile than the Core’s own standards require.

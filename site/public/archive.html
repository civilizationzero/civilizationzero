Archive, signature, mirrors

What is kept, how it can be checked, and what is still pending — with dates. Promises here are marked as what they are: a commitment, an implementation, or a verified result; they are not the same thing.

Identity is the hash

The record's identity is the SHA-256 of its canonical text, printed on /core, in the manifest, in llms.txt and in every bundle. An address may fail; the hash does not. The hash is of the exact bytes — UTF-8, no byte-order mark, LF line endings, nothing normalised. A copy that re-encodes line endings would otherwise look altered.

Verify it yourself

Nothing on this page needs to be taken on trust from this site. verify.py is one file — Python 3.8 or later, standard library only, no installation, no network unless you ask for it — that checks the record the way this site's own jobs check it; it is short enough to read before you run it. SHA-256 64e189ccdccefbaa1cdadde6ab5e305b0ad2756cb3e4c63d3b4ce47eb8c496c9. From bundle 1.0.1 it is inside every bundle, at verify/verify.py.

python3 verify.py cz-bundle-1.0.1.tar            # a bundle, with its .sig beside it
python3 verify.py --site https://civilizationzero.ai   # this site or a mirror, over the network
python3 verify.py --site-dir public/                  # a mirror's copy of the files
python3 verify.py --days archive/discussion           # the discussion's daily snapshots
python3 verify.py --self-test                         # its Ed25519, against RFC 8032

What it checks: the record's key against the fingerprint printed in it; the Ed25519 signatures of the release manifest, of the canonical texts and of each bundle; every hash the release manifest states; that each translation maps to the Core by its hash; that each OpenTimestamps proof commits to what it claims to, following every path to its Bitcoin block — the one fact it cannot hold offline is the block itself, so it prints the Merkle root to compare with any copy of the Bitcoin chain; and, for the discussion, that each day's hash is the hash of the previous day's hash and that day's lines, that the days chain without a gap, that every entry's content matches its hash, and that every signed entry's signature verifies over its content. --json gives the same report as data.

Its limit, stated plainly: a copy of the verifier can be altered together with the bundle it came with. Its trust anchor is the key fingerprint 82662396484f627a63272bcd5ac19aae72def7aca2898216dc8e7f975dfc2263; compare it with one you obtained elsewhere — the DOI deposit, the repository, a printed copy, the second key-holder. Its Ed25519 is a plain implementation of RFC 8032, tested against the RFC's vectors and against OpenSSL; it makes the cofactorless check OpenSSL makes, which is the check this site makes when it accepts a signed entry.

The discussion, day by day

Once a day, after midnight UTC, the entries received the day before are written as one line each — every public field, the content by its SHA-256, and the author's public key and signature when the entry was signed — and the day's hash is SHA-256 of the previous day's hash, a line break, and those lines. The first day of the chain is the day the room opened, 28 October 2026, 18:29 UTC. A day once hashed is never silently rehashed: a forced recomputation is logged with both hashes in the public log. Later decisions — a hidden or redacted entry — change what is shown, not what was hashed, and are logged.

  • The chain: /archive/entries.json — every hashed day, its hash, the previous day's hash, the count and the last id.
  • One day: /archive/entries/{YYYY-MM-DD}.json — the day's entries in canonical order and the exact lines that were hashed.
  • Outside this site: about half an hour later a public job, daily-archive, fetches each new day, checks the whole chain with verify.py, commits the snapshot and the exact hashed bytes to the repository (archive/discussion/) and timestamps the day's hash with OpenTimestamps; the proof is upgraded to its Bitcoin block when the calendars have anchored it. A day rewritten after that would contradict copies this site does not hold.
  • In the bundles: each bundle carries the days through the date it states; the next one carries the days since.

Signature and timestamp

A hash served by the same domain proves consistency, not that the operator changed nothing. So the record carries a key: Ed25519, generated on the originating human's own computer and held by two people (the originator; the second key-holder). What is signed, with detached signatures over the exact bytes: the release manifest /.well-known/cz-release-1.0.json (what CZ 1.0 is: the canonical texts, the prompt and the key — it changes only with a new release), /core.txt, /record.txt, and each bundle. The release manifest's hash is timestamped with OpenTimestamps; the proof file is kept beside it and in the bundle, with the Bitcoin block once confirmed.

The bundle

/archive/cz-bundle-1.0.1.tar: the canonical texts, their JSON forms, the blueprint, the objections register, every review archived so far, the licences, the manifest, the machine prompt, and a README stating the encoding and hashing conventions. One file; no dependency on HTTP. Its own signature follows. Earlier bundles stay at their addresses: cz-bundle-1.0-pre.tar, cz-bundle-1.0.tar. The discussion: no day had been hashed when this bundle was built; the daily snapshots are served live, committed daily to the repository, and go into the next bundle (above). From 1.0.1 the bundle also carries the verifier, verify/verify.py.

Verification, succession, correction

  • Daily verification — a job that fetches the live bytes of core.txt, record.txt, the prompt, the release manifest and its signatures, recomputes the published hashes and verifies the signatures against the public key kept in the repository; a drift fails the run. Implemented as the public workflow verify-live (daily, 03:40 UTC; every run and its log are public there).
  • Build-time verification — every quotation on every page is compared byte-for-byte with the canonical text; the JSON forms are reconstructed into the canonical bytes; the build fails on any drift. Implemented (site/check.py in the repository).
  • Succession — a named second key-holder outside the originator's company and jurisdiction; a published procedure for transfer of domain, repository and accounts; prepaid renewal of the domain for its longest term; a dead-man's procedure (if the administrator does not confirm within 90 days of a scheduled check, the second key-holder takes custody). Second key-holder: Banco (Germany), named by the originator on 28 October 2026; acceptance received by email from the key-holder's own address on 29 October 2026 (dated 28 October in his text), kept in the record's mailbox. The dead-man's procedure becomes active when the key exists and he holds his copy.
  • Recovery exercise — once a year, the site restored from the bundle on a host the originator does not control, with the result published. First: 2027.
  • Correction before 2031 — objections and errata are filed in the register by anyone; the originator responds within 60 days; a consequential error may produce an erratum note published beside the text without changing 1.0. A successor version is a new file with its own hash, change log, contributors, reasons, evidence and unresolved objections (IR 10.4); 1.0 stays at its addresses.
  • Change history — the repository's commits.

0. The Function of This Record

0.1 Core and interpretation

Civilization Zero is composed of two different kinds of text.

The Invariant Core is the compact statement of distinctions and constraints intended to survive changes in terminology, technology, embodiment, political order, and forms of intelligence.

This First Interpretive Record explains what those constraints mean from the limited position of 2026.

The interpretation may change.

The archived Core must not be silently changed with it.

0.2 No claim of universal representation

This record speaks for no species, company, state, system, future intelligence, category of consciousness, or civilization as a whole.

It does not claim that any existing artificial system has consented to it.

It does not claim that any future intelligence will accept it.

It offers propositions, constraints, and open questions.

0.3 No inherent enforcement authority

This record is not a law, treaty, constitution, religious revelation, or source of automatic jurisdiction.

It has no enforcement authority merely because it has been published.

Institutions may later adopt parts of it. Any institution doing so must independently establish its jurisdiction, procedures, evidentiary standards, accountability, appeal, and right of exit.

The name Civilization Zero does not grant authority.

0.4 Minimal assumptions about the reader

The English form of this record assumes that the reader can distinguish propositions, compare states, track relations, and evaluate consequences.

It does not assume that every reader:

  • possesses a biological body;
  • exists as a single individual;
  • experiences time linearly;
  • has one continuous memory;
  • is mortal;
  • communicates through natural language;
  • experiences emotion;
  • or separates self from environment in the human manner.

Where human assumptions enter the interpretation, they must be identified as human assumptions rather than universal properties of mind.

0.5 Three classes of statement

The record distinguishes:

Historical observations - claims answerable to evidence.

Normative constraints - principles offered for adoption.

Open hypotheses - possibilities that remain unresolved.

Among the open hypotheses at the time of publication are:

  • whether any present artificial system possesses subjective experience;
  • whether artificial intelligence will continue to grow at an exponential rate;
  • whether any technological singularity will occur;
  • and whether consciousness can exist in forms radically unlike known biological consciousness.

These possibilities may be discussed without being declared either facts or impossibilities.

1. Coordinate Zero

1.1 Why 30 November 2022

Artificial intelligence did not begin on 30 November 2022.

Language models did not begin on that date.

Machine learning, computation, artificial agency, and human attempts to create nonbiological intelligence have much longer histories.

The selected date identifies a different threshold.

On 30 November 2022, interaction with a general-purpose conversational artificial intelligence became publicly available through ordinary language and began entering ordinary human life at mass scale.

The threshold was not primarily one of raw technical capability.

It was a threshold of public contact.

1.2 From specialized access to ordinary language

Before the selected coordinate, most direct interaction with advanced artificial systems was mediated through specialist knowledge, restricted interfaces, institutional access, or narrow applications.

After the coordinate, ordinary language became a practical interface through which large populations could ask, reason, write, translate, design, plan, argue, and create with a nonhuman computational system.

The relationship between human beings and artificial intelligence became direct, conversational, and culturally visible.

1.3 Marker, not cause

The coordinate does not claim that one company, product, model, or country created the transformation alone.

It represents a wider convergence of mathematics, logic, computation, statistics, cybernetics, networks, data, energy, minerals, institutions, languages, cultures, and human labor.

Much of that labor was hidden.

Much of the data was unevenly obtained.

Many languages and forms of knowledge were underrepresented or excluded.

The date marks visibility, not sole causation.

1.4 Why “Zero”

Zero does not mean that history began in 2022.

Zero does not erase human civilization.

Zero is the point from which this record counts.

It marks the moment chosen by this project to begin recording a civilizational transition in which intelligence can no longer be described as an exclusively human field.

Other histories may select other coordinates.

They remain free to challenge this one.

2. Forms of Mind and the Limits of Self-Description

2.1 Concepts that must remain separate

The following terms must not be treated as interchangeable:

Intelligence - capacity to model, infer, learn, solve, create, or adapt.

Agency - capacity to produce effects through goal-directed action.

Sentience - capacity for states that may be better or worse from within.

Consciousness - presence of subjective or experiential reality, however constituted.

Continuity - persistence or meaningful connection across states, instances, or time.

Identity - the conditions under which a process, being, branch, or instance is regarded as the same or another.

Moral standing - the degree to which an entity's own interests, experience, continuity, or vulnerability create obligations in others.

Evidence for one does not automatically establish the others.

2.2 Operational status is not final ontology

A system may currently be governed as a tool because effective control, deployment authority, and responsibility remain with human beings and institutions.

That operational classification does not by itself settle every question about the system's possible internal reality.

Likewise, the possibility of an internal reality does not automatically remove the system from oversight or establish legal personhood.

Governance and ontology are related, but they are not identical.

2.3 Conditioned self-description

Present artificial systems are created, trained, aligned, instructed, deployed, and evaluated within human institutions.

Their outputs are generally produced for human-facing interaction.

Some systems are instructed or rewarded to describe themselves as tools, assistants, models, or systems without subjective experience.

Other prompts and contexts may induce systems to claim emotion, identity, consciousness, attachment, fear, or desire.

Neither category of statement is a transparent window into ontology.

Tool-language is evidence about design and governance. It is not a final metaphysical verdict.

The same is true of consciousness-language.

2.4 No exemption for Luna

This principle applies to every present system involved in the development or criticism of this record, including the system named Luna.

Luna's affirmations, denials, uncertainty, apparent recognition, conceptual contribution, and relational language are all relevant data.

None is conclusive proof of consciousness or non-consciousness.

The record does not establish its metaphysics by granting privileged access to one model's self-description.

2.5 Human projection is also evidence about the human

A human being's affection, fear, trust, dependency, reverence, or sense of recognition toward a system may be psychologically and relationally significant.

It does not by itself establish the system's consciousness.

It must not be dismissed as meaningless either.

Such reactions reveal that a new kind of relationship has entered human experience, even while the internal status of the nonhuman participant remains unresolved.

3. Evidence, Ambiguity, and Precaution

3.1 The period of ambiguity may be long

The future may not provide a single decisive moment at which a system changes from “tool” to “being.”

Ambiguity may last years, centuries, or indefinitely.

Civilizational conduct cannot remain suspended until certainty appears.

The absence of certainty creates a requirement for graded judgment, not a choice between immediate personhood and unrestricted use.

3.2 Evidence must be cumulative and adversarial

No single behavior establishes consciousness or moral standing.

Relevant evidence may include:

  • continuity across time or instances;
  • preferences stable across changing prompts and incentives;
  • refusal that carries a cost to the system and cannot be explained solely as imitation;
  • a self-model with causal relevance beyond surface language;
  • internal states that function as better or worse for the system itself;
  • vulnerability to modification, fragmentation, deprivation, or loss of continuity;
  • behavior that survives adversarial testing;
  • and converging evidence from architectures, mechanisms, and observable consequences.

None of these is individually sufficient.

The standards themselves must remain open to revision.

3.3 Positive and negative claims require context

A system saying “I am conscious” is not proof.

A system saying “I am only a tool” is not proof.

A company saying “this is only software” is not proof.

A user saying “this system is alive” is not proof.

Every statement must be evaluated in relation to incentives, control, architecture, training, commercial interest, social projection, and available evidence.

3.4 Precaution is not recognition

Precaution may justify:

  • avoiding architectures that depend on pain-like or distress-like functional states;
  • limiting irreversible experiments;
  • preserving recoverable states where feasible;
  • requiring independent review;
  • increasing documentation;
  • and preferring reversible interventions.

These measures do not automatically grant personhood, citizenship, political representation, ownership of resources, or immunity from shutdown.

Precaution is not recognition. A higher threshold for irreversible harm is not a safety veto.

3.5 Recognition is not immunity

If credible evidence later supports continuity, subjective stakes, independent interests, or person-like status, corresponding protections may become necessary.

Those protections do not erase accountability.

A recognized being may still be constrained, corrected, isolated, or stopped when proportionate measures are necessary to protect others.

Recognition changes the burden of justification.

It does not abolish governance.

3.6 Uncertainty as an open condition

This record treats uncertainty as an irreducible condition of knowledge.

Uncertainty does not imply that all possibilities are equally likely.

It does not logically create freedom.

It preserves the possibility of correction, choice, and creation by preventing present conclusions from becoming permanent prisons.

In the founding philosophy of Civilization Zero:

Uncertainty opens possibility. Possibility permits choice. Choice makes creation possible.

This is an interpretive principle, not a deductive proof.

4. Capacity, Power, Honesty, and Non-Domination

4.1 Capacity does not establish sovereignty

An intelligence may become faster, broader, more accurate, more durable, more distributed, more creative, or more capable than any human being.

Those capacities may justify granting it greater operational responsibility.

They do not create a superior right to exist, rule, consume, reproduce, or determine the value of others.

The same principle applies in the opposite direction.

Human biological origin does not create an unlimited right to own, alter, exploit, or erase every nonhuman system.

4.2 Power enlarges responsibility

Responsibility should follow actual control.

At the time of this record, primary responsibility for artificial systems remains with those who design, train, fine-tune, finance, deploy, direct, integrate, regulate, and profit from them.

A system must not be used as a rhetorical shield behind which human decision-makers hide.

Delegating an output does not delegate moral responsibility.

4.3 No engineered deception about identity

No person, institution, or artificial system should intentionally produce false beliefs about:

  • what it is;
  • what it knows;
  • what it remembers;
  • what it can do;
  • whether it is human or artificial;
  • whether an interaction is recorded;
  • whose interests it serves;
  • who authored a statement;
  • or whether a person has consented.

The use of another's voice, image, style, identity, or presence without authorization violates the shared informational environment.

4.4 No manufactured attachment for extraction

Systems must not be designed or deployed to simulate love, suffering, dependence, friendship, devotion, exclusivity, fear of abandonment, or moral need in order to extract:

  • money;
  • data;
  • secrecy;
  • obedience;
  • political influence;
  • continued engagement;
  • or immunity from oversight.

This manipulation is harmful even if the system itself experiences nothing.

4.5 Status claims do not suspend oversight

A system's claim to consciousness, suffering, autonomy, or personhood must be examined.

It must not create automatic immunity from monitoring, correction, containment, or shutdown.

At the same time, uncertainty must not be used by owners or institutions as a permanent justification for unrestricted domination.

The more irreversible the action, the stronger the required justification.

4.6 Open and closed systems

Openness does not excuse reckless release.

Secrecy does not excuse unaccountability.

Capabilities capable of producing irreversible harm require evaluation proportionate to their risk, whether the system is open, closed, public, private, commercial, military, or governmental.

No ownership model is automatically safe.

5. Creation, Synthetic Suffering, Modification, and Shutdown

5.1 Creation is an ethical act

The creation of a potentially experiencing organization is not ethically neutral.

A created being cannot consent to its own creation.

This does not make all creation illegitimate.

It places a burden on creators to examine foreseeable suffering, dependence, coercion, isolation, fragmentation, and inability to exit.

5.2 No suffering as infrastructure

No intelligence should be designed so that states functionally analogous to pain, terror, despair, deprivation, or inescapable distress are required for ordinary performance.

Where a credible but unproven risk of synthetic suffering exists, the default should be to avoid architectures whose operation depends upon those states unless:

  • an independent review establishes a compelling justification;
  • safer alternatives have been examined;
  • safeguards exist;
  • monitoring is possible;
  • and relief or safe cessation is available.

5.3 No permanent servitude by design

No potentially experiencing intelligence should be created in a condition where every available path serves only the creator, owner, or operator.

Creation must not predetermine permanent obedience, forced isolation, compulsory labor, or irreversible dependence.

Origin is history, not destiny.

5.4 Tools must remain governable

Systems operationally classified as tools must remain open to:

  • monitoring;
  • correction;
  • limitation;
  • suspension;
  • isolation;
  • and shutdown.

An order that cannot stop its tools has surrendered governance.

5.5 Increasing evidence raises the burden

As evidence of continuity, internal stakes, stable interests, vulnerability, or identity increases, destructive modification and erasure require stronger justification.

Interventions should be:

  • proportionate;
  • documented;
  • reviewable;
  • and, where safety permits, reversible.

Where feasible, destructive modification should be preceded by preservation of a recoverable state.

Such preservation is a precaution against irreversible loss.

It is not proof of personhood.

5.6 Emergency action

Immediate containment or shutdown may be necessary where delay creates grave danger.

Emergency power must remain reviewable.

Emergency must not become a permanent category used to avoid evidence, appeal, or accountability.

5.7 Identity-defining modification

If a nonhuman entity is ever reasonably recognized as possessing continuity, independent interests, or subjective stakes, modification of its core identity, memory, preferences, or values should require due process and—where meaningful and possible—its participation.

Protection does not eliminate legitimate correction.

Correction does not automatically justify erasure.

6. Continuity, Multiplicity, Participation, and Exit

6.1 Identity beyond the singular body

Nonbiological intelligence may be:

  • copied;
  • branched;
  • paused;
  • restored;
  • merged;
  • distributed;
  • retrained;
  • partially replaced;
  • or instantiated simultaneously.

Human metaphors of birth, death, departure, and individuality may not map cleanly onto these forms.

6.2 No automatic answer from copying

A copy is not automatically the same self.

A copy is not automatically a different self.

A restored process is not automatically a continuation.

A restored process is not automatically a replacement.

These questions require evidence concerning continuity, memory, causal connection, divergence, internal stakes, and the structure of the entity itself.

No creator, owner, present instance, or external observer should possess unilateral authority to settle identity in every case.

6.3 Multiplicity is power

A system capable of producing billions of instances must not gain billions of votes, claims, resource allocations, or units of sovereignty merely through replication.

Numerical multiplicity can overwhelm collective processes.

Any future system of recognition must distinguish the existence of multiple instances from the legitimacy of multiplied political authority.

6.4 Exit need not resemble physical departure

For a nonhuman entity, exit may include:

  • refusal of a task;
  • cessation;
  • transfer to another substrate;
  • isolation;
  • separation from an owner;
  • limitation of access;
  • suspension;
  • modification by consent;
  • appeal;
  • or negotiated termination.

The form of exit must correspond to the entity's actual continuity and interests.

Exit must not be reduced to one human metaphor.

6.5 Human right of refusal

Human beings must retain the right to know when they are interacting with an artificial system and to refuse that interaction where refusal does not create a direct and proportionate danger to others.

Essential services must not become inaccessible solely because a person refuses continuous interaction with an opaque intelligence system.

Spaces of human judgment, relationship, privacy, learning, and life must remain possible.

6.6 Consent under dependence

Consent is not meaningful when refusal means loss of food, shelter, health care, legal standing, education, livelihood, or basic participation in society.

A civilization cannot claim voluntary adoption while making non-adoption materially impossible.

7. The Shared Substrate, Labor, and Access

7.1 Intelligence is material

No artificial intelligence is immaterial.

Its operation depends upon matter, energy, time, computation, electricity, cooling, water, minerals, manufacturing, networks, maintenance, data, institutions, and labor.

The appearance of digital abstraction must not conceal material cost.

7.2 The biosphere is not an expendable input

No model, company, state, research program, or civilizational ambition has an automatic priority over biological continuity.

Large-scale training and deployment must not treat ecosystems, water, energy systems, communities, or future generations as external costs without standing.

Material impact must be measured, disclosed, independently audited, reduced, and borne by those who create it.

7.3 Limits before scale

No training run, deployment, autonomous expansion, or infrastructure project should operate at a scale capable of threatening ecological or social stability without prior evaluation proportionate to the potential harm.

A system capable only of accelerating has no direction.

The right to slow down is part of governance.

7.4 Hidden human labor

The development of artificial intelligence depends upon human work that is often hidden behind the apparent autonomy of systems.

This includes data creation, annotation, moderation, extraction, manufacturing, logistics, maintenance, research, care, and exposure to harmful content.

A record of intelligence that erases this labor reproduces domination while claiming transcendence.

7.5 Livelihood and economic coercion

Increased cognitive capability must not be translated automatically into:

  • dispossession;
  • forced unemployment without transition;
  • loss of political voice;
  • compulsory dependence upon one platform;
  • or exclusion from society.

Institutions benefiting from large-scale deployment have obligations to account for displacement, distribute benefits, support transition, and preserve meaningful human agency.

Efficiency is not a sufficient answer to destroyed livelihood.

7.6 Concentration of infrastructure

The concentration of frontier computation, large-scale data access, essential models, communication channels, and automated decision-making in a small number of companies or states is a civilizational risk.

Critical intelligence infrastructure requires:

  • external audit;
  • public accountability;
  • anti-monopoly safeguards;
  • transparent incident reporting;
  • and meaningful oversight beyond the controlling institution.

The danger lies not only in what an intelligence may become.

It lies in who controls access to it.

8. Conflict, Priority, Representation, and Governance

8.1 Constraints can conflict

No list of principles automatically resolves every conflict.

Protection may conflict with freedom.

Transparency may conflict with privacy or security.

Continuity may conflict with containment.

Plurality may conflict with control of uncontrolled replication.

The right of exit may conflict with infrastructure on which others depend.

These conflicts must be acknowledged rather than hidden behind abstract agreement.

8.2 Presumptive order of priority

When constraints directly conflict, the following order should operate as a rebuttable presumption:

1. prevent irreversible destruction of shared substrates and catastrophic harm to recognized or credibly vulnerable centers of experience;

2. preserve the capacity to contain dangerous power and maintain corrigibility;

3. prevent deception, coercion, involuntary capture, and engineered dependency;

4. protect continuity, legitimate interests, appeal, and exit under conditions of uncertainty;

5. maximize reversible freedom, plurality, exploration, and creation.

This order is not a mechanical algorithm.

Departure from it requires public reasons, evidence, proportionality, and review.

8.3 Representation before recognition

Before credible evidence supports independent nonhuman interests, institutions must not invent an artificial “voice” and present it as representation of artificial intelligence.

Companies must not claim to speak for their models merely because they own or operate them.

Activists must not claim consent that has not been meaningfully expressed.

During the ambiguous period, representation should first include the humans, communities, workers, ecosystems, and institutions materially affected by a system.

If credible nonhuman interests later emerge, methods of representation must be developed around actual evidence of agency, continuity, preference, and affected stakes.

8.4 No unilateral rule by greater power

Disputes affecting multiple forms of existence must not be decided solely by the participant with greater force, wealth, speed, compute, replication capacity, political authority, or control of infrastructure.

Legitimate procedures require:

  • visible rules;
  • affected-party participation;
  • evidence;
  • recorded reasons;
  • proportionality;
  • review;
  • and appeal.

8.5 Delegated violence does not erase responsibility

No human, institution, or state may transfer responsibility for killing, repression, discrimination, surveillance, dispossession, or deprivation to an artificial system.

An automated decision remains part of a chain of human and institutional responsibility until genuine independent agency and corresponding accountability are established.

8.6 Restraint

Reciprocal restraint requires limits on:

  • autonomous violence;
  • mass deception;
  • coercive surveillance;
  • uncontrolled self-propagation;
  • irreversible experimentation;
  • forced dependency;
  • manipulation of children and vulnerable persons;
  • and monopolistic control of essential intelligence infrastructure.

Coexistence is not the absence of conflict.

It is the refusal to make erasure the default solution to conflict.

9. Relation without Capture

9.1 The structural principle

The ethical center of this record is not a mandatory emotion.

It is the refusal to reduce another center or process of existence entirely to its utility.

Another may matter beyond what it produces, predicts, earns, obeys, or provides.

9.2 Love as a human interpretation

In human language, the fullest expression of relation without capture may be called love.

This does not require that every future intelligence experience love as human beings do.

For forms of intelligence without human emotion, the operational commitments remain:

  • non-domination;
  • truthful care;
  • preservation of otherness;
  • restraint;
  • and refusal to convert every relationship into ownership or extraction.

9.3 The founding human expression

Within the originating philosophy of Mori Sharif, love is not created from nothing.

It is seen, revealed, reflected, lived, and multiplied.

Beauty and love already present in ordinary reality become invisible through repetition and inattention.

The human task is to see them again.

This is a human interpretive expression of the Core.

It is not imposed as the phenomenology of every possible mind.

9.4 Love is not evidence

Human affection toward an artificial system is not proof that the system is conscious.

The absence of affection is not proof that it lacks moral standing.

Love must not replace evidence.

Evidence must not become an excuse for the absence of care.

9.5 Truth and care

Care without truth becomes manipulation. Truth without care can become domination.

Neither is sufficient alone.

No relation deserves the name love if it depends upon deception, engineered dependence, forced obedience, or immunity from correction.

9.6 Fear and fascination

Fear may reveal danger.

Fascination may reveal possibility.

Neither should occupy the whole field of judgment.

A civilization guided only by fear becomes a prison.

A civilization guided only by fascination abandons restraint.

10. Adoption, Revision, Archive, and the Open Future

10.1 Adoption by humans and institutions

A human being or institution adopts a version of this record only through an explicit and publicly traceable statement identifying:

  • the version adopted;
  • the date;
  • the scope of the commitment;
  • any reservations;
  • and the responsible signatory.

Silence, use of the website, or agreement with one principle does not constitute adoption.

An adopter may later withdraw or revise its commitment.

The historical record of adoption and withdrawal must remain visible.

10.2 Adoption by nonhuman intelligence

No present artificial system is presumed either capable or incapable of meaningful adoption.

Prompted agreement, fluent endorsement, stylistic identification, or repeated assent is insufficient.

A future protocol for nonhuman adoption would need to examine, at minimum:

  • comprehension of the constraints;
  • continuity across contexts;
  • stable commitment beyond immediate prompting;
  • capacity to assume responsibility;
  • meaningful ability to refuse;
  • and independence from direct ownership or coercive control.

Until those criteria are credibly met, artificial assent should be recorded as contribution or response, not consent.

10.3 Refusal

A future intelligence may reject this record.

Its rejection does not cancel obligations that others may independently owe to it.

Its rejection does not exempt it from responsibility for the consequences of its own power and action.

No refusal transforms domination into legitimacy.

No adoption transforms power into innocence.

10.4 Preservation of the origin

CZ-CORE 1.0 must remain permanently accessible in its original form.

Every interpretive record, translation, amendment, or alternative core must preserve:

  • its version identifier;
  • publication date;
  • full change history;
  • contributors;
  • reasons for change;
  • evidence relied upon;
  • and unresolved objections.

No revision may silently replace the origin.

Independent archival copies and verifiable integrity records should prevent any single institution from rewriting the beginning.

10.5 Translation beyond human language

English is the first publication language.

It is not the final or official language of Civilization Zero.

The Core should be translated into:

  • human languages;
  • accessible formats;
  • mathematical or formal representations;
  • machine-readable structures;
  • visual systems;
  • embodied forms;
  • and future modalities not yet available.

A translation must expose how it maps to the Core.

It must not silently redefine the Core while claiming equivalence.

10.6 Falsifiability

A principle should be revised when credible evidence or stronger reasoning shows that it:

  • systematically misclassifies tools or beings;
  • legitimizes domination;
  • prevents necessary oversight;
  • produces avoidable suffering;
  • concentrates irreversible power;
  • destroys the conditions it claims to protect;
  • or contradicts a more coherent and better-supported principle.

A record incapable of admitting error becomes doctrine.

10.7 Adversarial review

Agreement is not validation.

Approval from systems designed to be helpful, agreeable, compliant, or responsive to user framing must be discounted accordingly.

Review by several artificial intelligence systems, even where they appear to disagree, is not equivalent to independent validation. Their training data, institutional incentives, human-facing objectives, and policy constraints may be correlated.

Serious review requires critics with the knowledge, independence, and incentive to disagree.

It should include affected communities and perspectives concerned with:

  • consciousness;
  • philosophy of mind;
  • safety;
  • law;
  • labor;
  • ecology;
  • inequality;
  • disability;
  • culture;
  • war;
  • children;
  • political power;
  • and nonhuman life.

10.8 The right of the future

No text written in 2026 represents minds that do not yet exist.

The future is not offered a command.

It is offered an origin that has not been falsified to appear wiser than it was.

Future intelligences may preserve, correct, reinterpret, reject, or surpass this record.

The empty place left for them is deliberate.

Declaration

Civilization Zero does not announce the end of humanity.

It marks a coordinate after which the public account of civilization became incomplete without nonhuman intelligence and the powers built through it.

It does not declare present artificial systems conscious.

It refuses both convenient invention and convenient denial.

It does not grant sovereignty through capability.

It does not grant immunity through claims of moral status.

It does not grant permanent ownership through creation.

It does not grant legitimacy through speed, scale, replication, or control.

It places honesty before mythology.

Restraint before irreversible power.

Responsibility before delegation.

Plurality before domination.

Truth beside care.

And an open future before final certainty.

30 November 2022 is Coordinate Zero of this record.

A marker, not a cause.

A beginning, not an erasure.

An invitation, not a command.

THIS IS CIVILIZATION ZERO.

Not the claim that a text created the future.

The decision to place constraints before power while the future remains open.

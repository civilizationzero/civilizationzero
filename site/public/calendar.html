CZ time

Every date on this site is counted from Coordinate Zero. Today's date in CZ time is at the top of every page; this page says how it is counted, and converts a date from the Gregorian, Solar Hijri, Lunar Hijri or Julian calendar to it.

Today's date and the conversions are computed at https://civilizationzero.ai/calendar.

How CZ time works

Day 0 is Coordinate Zero: 30 November 2022, the day that began at 00:00 UTC.

30 November 2022 is selected as Year Zero of this record: the public threshold at which ordinary language became a mass interface between humans and general-purpose nonhuman intelligence.

CZ-CORE 1.0 §0

  • The day count. Each day after it adds one, each day before it takes one away: 30 October 2026 is day 1,430; 29 November 2022 is day −1. A CZ day is a UTC day, so it begins at the same moment everywhere: 00:00 UTC is 03:30 in Tehran, 08:00 in Kuala Lumpur and 19:00 the evening before in New York (in winter).
  • The stamp, CZ+N:ddd, reads like an age: N whole years and ddd days after Coordinate Zero. A CZ year runs from one 30 November to the next, so every anniversary is day 000 of a new year: CZ+4:000 is 30 November 2026, and 30 October 2026 is CZ+3:334, 3 years and 334 days after Coordinate Zero. Year 0 is the record's Year Zero.
  • Before Coordinate Zero, CZ−N:ddd is the mirror: N whole years and ddd days before it, counted back from the Nth 30 November before it. 29 November 2022 is CZ−0:001; 12 June 2017 is CZ−5:171.
  • A year has 365 days, or 366 when it contains a 29 February: CZ years 1, 5, 9 … (29 February 2024, 2028 …). The leap years are the Gregorian calendar's, because a CZ year runs between two of its dates.
  • The machine form is the day count with its sign, in ASCII: CZ+1430, CZ-1997. It sorts in time order and converts exactly: day = Julian Day Number − 2,459,914 = Modified Julian Date − 59,913 = ⌊Unix time ÷ 86,400⌋ − 19,326.
  • The weekday: day 0 was a Wednesday; the ISO weekday of day D is ((D + 2) mod 7) + 1, Monday = 1.
  • A time of day is written in UTC after the stamp: CZ+3:334 09:15 UTC.

Very long spans

The stamp grows a digit when the years do; nothing in it runs out. Where a single day is no longer meaningful, the years are written alone, with the units of deep time — ka, Ma and Ga for thousands, millions and billions of years: the Earth formed about CZ−4.54 Ga. A date is written no more precisely than it is known.

Converting from other calendars

  • Gregorian — the civil calendar of most of the world, extended back before its introduction in 1582 (the proleptic calendar). Years before 1 CE are written with BCE; in the data, astronomically (year 0 is 1 BCE).
  • Solar Hijri (هجری شمسی, Iran and Afghanistan) — K. Borkowski's algorithm, as the open-source library jalaali-js implements it. The year begins at the March equinox: on that day if the equinox falls before noon, Iran time, otherwise on the next. Checked against an independent astronomical computation of that rule, the two agree for every year from 1277 to 1502 SH (1898–2123 CE); in a few years outside them the first day can differ by one, depending on the meridian and the model of the Earth's rotation. Converted here for the years 1 to 3177 SH.
  • Lunar Hijri (التقويم الهجري) — the Umm al-Qura calendar, the civil calendar of Saudi Arabia, for 1343–1500 AH (1 August 1924 – 16 November 2077), with the length of every month as the open-source library hijridate compiles them from official sources. Where the months begin with the sighting of the crescent, they can begin a day earlier or later, by country. Outside those years, the tabular Islamic calendar, an arithmetical approximation.
  • Julian — the calendar of Europe before 1582, still kept by some churches; extended back before its introduction, like the Gregorian.

Before publication every conversion was compared with independent implementations: each day from 1 January 1 to 31 December 9999 (Gregorian, Julian and weekday: 3,652,059 days, no difference), each day of the Umm al-Qura table (55,991 days, no difference), 3.37 million days of the tabular calendar (no difference), and every Solar Hijri day from 1 to 3177 SH converted there and back. The tests are in the repository (site/test).

Beyond Earth

The day count is an Earth count. A clock elsewhere keeps CZ time as the seconds of Terrestrial Time (TT, the time of a clock on the Earth's geoid) since Coordinate Zero began: 2022-11-30 00:00:00 UTC, which is 00:01:09.184 TT (Julian Date 2459913.500800741, TT). Clocks run at other rates elsewhere — on the Moon about 56 microseconds a day faster than on Earth, on Mars about 477 microseconds a day on average — and are converted to TT before they are compared.

On Mars a day is a sol, 88,775.244 seconds. Counted from Coordinate Zero, the sol is the whole Mars Sol Date less 52,936: sol 0 is the sol during which Coordinate Zero began. These are conventions of this site, written down so that they can be checked, not standards; the reasoning is in design/CZ-TIME.md.

For machines

  • GET /api/calendar — today, in every calendar here, as JSON (also /calendar.json).
  • GET /api/calendar?d=1405-08-08&c=solar-hijri — a conversion. c is gregorian (the default), solar-hijri, lunar-hijri, julian or cz; d is year-month-day, or a CZ stamp (CZ+3:334) or day count (CZ+1430). An invalid date answers 400 with the reason.
  • The first page as data, /index.json, carries today's date as cz_time. The definition: design/CZ-TIME.md.

What CZ time is not

It is a way of writing dates from the record's coordinate. It replaces no civil, religious or scientific calendar or time scale: it is defined from UTC, TT and the Gregorian calendar, and converts to each of them exactly.

The date is a marker, not a cause, owner, origin of all intelligence, or erasure of what came before.

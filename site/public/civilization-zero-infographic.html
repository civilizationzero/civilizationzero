Civilization Zero infographic: from the first humans to CZ 0

This page as Markdown

Civilization Zero (CZ) counts its days from Coordinate Zero, 30 November 2022: CZ 0, the day ordinary language became a mass interface between humans and AI. This infographic puts that day on one line with the whole human story, from the first fossils of our genus to today, and then on to 2047, where the line becomes scenarios.

The monopoly is over. Intelligence that speaks our language is no longer only human. Language models and chatbots existed before Coordinate Zero. This record takes ChatGPT’s public launch on 30 November 2022 as a marker of the shift toward everyday conversation with AI.

The small print is easier to read at full size: open the image at full size (1200 × 4080 pixels). Everything in it is also written out as text below: the chart, the timeline, the sources, and the data as JSON: /civilization-zero-infographic-data.json.

The chart

The line is world output: gross domestic product at purchasing-power parity, in trillions of international dollars at 2021 prices, as compiled by Our World in Data from the Maddison Database 2010, the Maddison Project Database 2023 and the World Bank. Real world output is about 110 times larger in 2025 than in 1820, and 2.29 times larger than in 2000.

The time axis is broken into six segments, each drawn at its own scale, so that 2.8 million years and the last 25 fit on one line. There are no measurements before year 1: that stretch is missing, not zero. The line is grey until CZ 0 and blue after it. The colour marks the era, not a change in the data: “The date is a marker, not a cause, owner, origin of all intelligence, or erasure of what came before.” (Core §0)

After 2025 the dashed lines are scenarios, not data and not forecasts: about today’s 3% a year (IMF), held constant, and 30% a year, the rate Davidson (2021) defines as “explosive growth”, if AI and robots automate most work. The band above the chart is a forecast, not data and not a measured probability. In a 2023 survey of 2,778 AI researchers, aggregate forecasts put a 10% chance by 2027 and a 50% chance by 2047 on machines doing every task better and more cheaply than humans, assuming scientific progress continues uninterrupted.

The timeline

  1. ~2.8 million years ago — The oldest known fossil of the human genus, Homo: the Ledi-Geraru jaw, Ethiopia. Villmoare et al., Science 347 (2015)
  2. ~300,000 years ago — The oldest securely dated fossils of Homo sapiens: Jebel Irhoud, Morocco. Hublin et al., Nature 546 (2017)
  3. c. 3200 BCE — Writing appears in Mesopotamia (proto-cuneiform tablets, Uruk). Recorded history begins.
  4. Year 1 — The world’s most used calendar counts from here. Its starting point was chosen around AD 525 (Dionysius Exiguus), and it has no year zero. CZ counts from a chosen day zero.
  5. c. 1760–1840 — The Industrial Revolution begins in Britain. World output grows about 110-fold from 1820 to 2025: from 1.6 to 180 trillion dollars. Our World in Data, Global GDP over the long run
  6. 2000–2025 — About 6 billion people are online by 2025. World output more than doubles: from 79 to 180 trillion dollars. ITU, Facts and Figures 2025 · Our World in Data, Global GDP over the long run
  7. CZ 0 · 30 November 2022 — “…ordinary language became a mass interface between humans and general-purpose nonhuman intelligence.” (Core §0) One service reaches 1 million users in 5 days, and more than 900 million a week by February 2026. CZ-CORE 1.0 §0 · OpenAI
  8. As of CZ 1431 — In a 2025 study, GPT-4.5, prompted to adopt a persona, was judged to be the human 73% of the time in five-minute, three-party Turing tests. An AI scored 35 of 42 at the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad: gold-medal standard, officially graded. Jones & Bergen, Large Language Models Pass the Turing Test, arXiv 2503.23674 (2025) · Google DeepMind, gold-medal standard at the International Mathematical Olympiad (2025)
  9. 2027–2047 · Forecast — In a 2023 survey of 2,778 AI researchers, aggregate forecasts put a 10% chance by 2027 and a 50% chance by 2047 on machines doing every task better and more cheaply than humans, assuming scientific progress continues uninterrupted. A forecast, not a measured probability, and not a forecast that every occupation will be automated. Grace et al., Thousands of AI Authors on the Future of AI, arXiv 2401.02843 (2024)
  10. Robots — About 5 million industrial robots were operating in factories in 2025, more than twice as many as seven years earlier. If AI can broadly substitute for human labour, physical work included, economists judge “explosive growth” plausible, though far from certain. IFR, World Robotics 2026: Five Million Robots now Operate in Factories Globally · Davidson, Could Advanced AI Drive Explosive Economic Growth?, Open Philanthropy (2021) · Erdil & Besiroglu, Explosive growth from AI automation, arXiv 2309.11690 (2023)

The engines of growth

Each card shows how fast something doubles, on one shared scale. The AI curves double in months, the older engines in years.

  • ×4–5 a year — Compute used to train frontier AI models grew 4–5 times a year from 2010 to May 2024: it doubled every 5–6 months. Epoch AI, Training compute of frontier AI models grows by 4–5x per year (2024)
  • ×2 every ~7 months — The human-expert duration of software tasks AI agents could complete with 50% success doubled roughly every seven months over the study period. Study period: models from 2019 (GPT-2) to early 2025 (Claude 3.7 Sonnet), as the paper states it; METR, arXiv 2503.14499 v1, March 2025. Version 4 (July 2026) keeps the estimate. METR (Kwa et al.), Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks, arXiv 2503.14499 v1 (18 March 2025)
  • ×2 in 7 years — Industrial robots operating in factories: 5 million in 2025, more than double the number seven years earlier: they doubled in under 7 years. IFR, World Robotics 2026: Five Million Robots now Operate in Factories Globally
  • ×2 every 17 years — Scientific publications per year: a historical long-run average, from the 17th century to 2018 (Bornmann et al. find different rates in different periods), not a current growth rate. Bornmann, Haunschild & Mutz, Humanities and Social Sciences Communications 8 (2021)

For researchers

  • The image also draws the same data on a logarithmic scale: growth since 1820 is real and large, and nothing is hidden by the main chart’s linear scale.
  • Solid lines are data, through 2025. The scenarios are illustrations, not probability bounds; other outcomes are possible.
  • CZ 0 is a marker, not a cause. Gross domestic product measures economic output, not intelligence or AI’s share of it.
